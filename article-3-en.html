Hypnotic Scripts and Autogenic Training for Somatized Depression and Anxiety Disorders

Author: Inessa Vladimirovna Simakina (Zaitseva) — Psychologist, Research Fellow, East-European Institute of Psychoanalysis (EEIP), Saint Petersburg

Abstract

This article explores the history of psychosomatic medicine in the context of psychoanalysis, psychology, and hypnosis. The author presents clinical case studies from private practice illustrating successful treatment of somatized (masked) depression, generalized anxiety disorders, psychogenic bulimia, obesity, chronic back pain, and dermatological reactions. Hypnotic scripts, anchoring protocols, and autogenic training techniques aimed at enhancing self-esteem, reducing traumatization, and restoring vitality are outlined.

Keywords: psychosomatics, hypnosis, anxiety disorder, somatized depression, autogenic training, self-esteem.

Psychosomatic Mechanisms: From Freud and Alexander to Reich

Psychosomatics emerged as an independent scientific discipline in the early 20th century through the synthesis of psychoanalysis and somatic medicine. Sigmund Freud introduced the concept of conversion hysteria, showing how blocked emotions discharge through bodily symptoms. Franz Alexander demonstrated that chronic unconscious emotional tension triggers autonomic dysregulation in vulnerable organ systems, while Walter Cannon described the sympathetic fight-or-flight response. Wilhelm Reich further linked chronic emotional repression to muscular 'character armor' and the somatic core of neurosis.

Clinical Manifestations of Somatized Depression and Anxiety

Somatized ('masked') depression hides behind autonomic cardiovascular complaints, chronic seasonal musculoskeletal pain, globus hystericus, dysomnia, and unexplained fatigue. Generalized anxiety disorder reinforces these symptoms through somatic sensitivity and fear of losing control. Clinical cases demonstrate that addressing underlying childhood trauma via rescripting, Dilts's Healing Trance, and Elias's weight-regulation scripts leads to lasting remission of bulimia, psychogenic obesity, and stress-induced hypertension or glycemic fluctuations.

Autogenic Training, Self-Hypnosis, and Anchoring

Structured self-regulation reinforces clinical outcomes between sessions. Johannes Schultz's Autogenic Training (six standard exercises targeting heaviness, warmth, cardiac and respiratory regulation, solar plexus warmth, and forehead coolness), Edmund Jacobson's Progressive Muscle Relaxation, and 12-formula self-hypnosis protocols empower patients to regulate autonomic arousal. Combined with NLP anchoring and transderivational search, these tools establish durable emotional resilience.
